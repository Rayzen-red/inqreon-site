Microsoft Copilot is the assistant you meet whether you look for it or not: in Windows, in Edge, in Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook. On the web it looks small. Similarweb counted about 80.1 million visits to copilot.microsoft.com and copilot.com in October 2026, seventh among AI chatbots, according to [Momentic](https://momenticmarketing.com/blog/top-ai-chatbots). But those figures only count the website. Most people use Copilot inside apps they already pay for, and that use doesn't show up in web traffic.

So the useful question isn't "is Copilot popular," it is "which Microsoft 365 plan gives me the Copilot I need." Here is the breakdown.

## What Copilot is

Copilot is Microsoft's name for its AI assistant across products. There are two sides to it:

- **The Copilot app** for chat, image creation, voice and help with what's on your screen. A free version exists; paid Microsoft 365 plans raise its limits.
- **Copilot inside Microsoft 365 apps**: Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and OneNote. This is where it drafts documents, builds slides, analyzes spreadsheets and summarizes email.

For consumers, Microsoft now sells paid Copilot features as part of Microsoft 365 Personal, Family and Premium, per the [plan comparison page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/buy/compare-all-microsoft-365-products). For companies, there are separate business plans and a Copilot add-on.

## Where it is strong

**It works where Office files live.** If your week is Word documents, Excel sheets and PowerPoint decks, Copilot can act on them in place. You don't export a file to a chatbot and paste the result back.

**Bundled with things you may already buy.** Microsoft 365 Personal and Family include desktop Office apps, 1 TB or more of OneDrive storage and Microsoft Defender security, with Copilot on top. If you'd pay for Office anyway, the AI part is close to free.

**Business controls.** For companies, Microsoft 365 Copilot sits inside the same admin, security and compliance setup the IT team already manages. That is often the deciding factor at work.

## Where it falls short

**Plans are confusing.** Personal, Family, Premium, Business Standard with Copilot, Business Premium with Copilot, the Copilot Business add-on, and enterprise plans: it takes real effort to work out which one you need.

**AI for the owner only on Family.** Microsoft notes that on Microsoft 365 Family, AI features are for the subscription owner only, even though the plan covers up to six people.

**Limits are described loosely.** Personal and Family offer "higher usage than free." Premium offers "extensive usage." Microsoft doesn't give a number on the page.

**Promotions blur the real price.** At the time of checking, several plans showed a discounted first month or first year. The regular price kicks in at renewal unless you cancel.

**Less of a standalone pick.** Outside Microsoft's apps, Copilot competes with ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, which each have broader standalone toolsets.

## What it is for

- Drafting and rewriting documents in Word.
- Building a first draft of a PowerPoint deck from notes.
- Asking questions about a spreadsheet in Excel: trends, formulas, charts.
- Summarizing long email threads in Outlook.
- Everyday chat and image creation in the Copilot app.

## Who it suits

**The free Copilot app** suits anyone on Windows who wants a quick assistant without paying.

**Microsoft 365 Personal or Family** fits people who already want Office and storage and would like Copilot inside Word and Excel as a bonus.

**Microsoft 365 Premium** is for individuals who lean on Copilot heavily: Microsoft lists extensive usage and agentic features, including advanced research and analytics.

**Business plans** are for companies whose work already runs on Microsoft 365.

## Pricing and plans (as of November 2026)

US prices from Microsoft's official [plan comparison page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/buy/compare-all-microsoft-365-products) and [Copilot business pricing page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/pricing), checked on November 15, 2026. Regular prices are shown; Microsoft was also running limited-time discounts.

**For individuals**

| Plan | Regular price | Highlights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copilot app | Free | Chat, images, voice, with lower limits |
| Microsoft 365 Personal | $9.99/month or $99.99/year | 1 person, Office desktop apps with Copilot, higher Copilot usage than free, 1 TB storage |
| Microsoft 365 Family | $12.99/month or $129.99/year | Up to 6 people, 2 TB shared storage, AI for the subscription owner only |
| Microsoft 365 Premium | $19.99/month or $199.99/year | Everything in Family plus extensive Copilot usage and agentic features |

**For business** (per user, per month, paid yearly)

| Plan | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Microsoft 365 Copilot Business (add-on) | From $21.00, shown at $18.00 during a promotion |
| Microsoft 365 Business Standard with Copilot | $23.50 |
| Microsoft 365 Business Premium with Copilot | $32.00 |

Monthly billing for business plans costs more, and Microsoft notes usage-based billing for select advanced AI features. Prices vary by country, and some business products aren't sold in every market.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Works directly in Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook
- Comes bundled with Office apps and storage
- Fits existing company security and admin setups
- Free app for casual use

**Cons**

- A maze of plans and add-ons
- AI on Family is for the owner only
- Usage limits aren't given as numbers
- Promo prices hide the renewal price

## The short verdict

If you already pay for Microsoft 365, open Copilot in Word and Excel this week and see if it saves you time before you pay more. If you don't use Office much, a standalone assistant will likely give you more for the money. For companies on Microsoft 365, the business plans make sense mostly because they fit the setup you already have.
