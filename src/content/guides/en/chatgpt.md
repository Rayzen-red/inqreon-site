ChatGPT is still the AI assistant most people open first. In October 2026 chatgpt.com drew about 6.05 billion web visits, according to Similarweb figures compiled by [Momentic](https://momenticmarketing.com/blog/top-ai-chatbots). That is more than Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek, Grok, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot combined. The question for most people is no longer whether to try it, but whether to pay for it, and which plan to pick.

This review covers what ChatGPT is good at, where it gets in the way, and how the four personal plans differ.

## What ChatGPT is

ChatGPT is OpenAI's chat assistant. You type or talk, and it answers, writes, edits, reasons through problems, reads files and images, searches the web and creates pictures. It runs in the browser, on iOS and Android, and in desktop apps.

Over the past year it has grown well past a chat box. The [pricing page](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/) now lists deep research, projects, scheduled tasks, custom GPTs, memory across chats, Codex for programming work and ChatGPT Work, which OpenAI offers on desktop, web and mobile. The current model family is GPT-6. Plus subscribers get GPT-6.1 Sol and advanced reasoning, and Pro adds a reasoning mode OpenAI calls GPT-6 Astra.

## Where it is strong

**Breadth.** ChatGPT is a generalist. The same window can draft an email, explain a lease clause, turn a photo of a whiteboard into a task list and write a small script. If you only want one AI subscription, this range is the main argument for it.

**Low friction.** The free plan lets you start without a card, and OpenAI says everyday text chats are unlimited on every plan, subject to its abuse guardrails. Most people can get real work done before they think about money.

**Tools in one place.** Image creation, voice chats, file uploads and deep research all sit in the same app. You don't have to stitch three services together to go from research to a draft to a picture for the post.

**Long context on paid plans.** The pricing page lists a 256K token reasoning context window on Go and Plus and 400K on Pro. In plain terms, you can hand it long documents and keep long conversations going without the model losing the thread as fast.

## Where it falls short

**Limits are vague.** The free plan comes with "limited" uploads, images, voice, research and memory. Paid plans are "expanded" or "maximum." OpenAI does not publish exact counts for most of these, so you find the ceiling by hitting it.

**Ads on Go.** OpenAI notes that the Go plan may include ads. If you want a quiet workspace, that matters.

**Training on your content by default.** The comparison table on the pricing page says content on personal plans is used to train OpenAI's models, with an opt-out. Turn it off in settings if you paste client material or private notes.

**Confident mistakes.** Like every chatbot, ChatGPT can state wrong facts in a calm voice. Ask for sources when the answer matters, and check numbers yourself.

## What it is for

Here is where ChatGPT earns its place in a normal week:

- Writing and editing: emails, posts, product pages, cover letters, rewrites in a different tone.
- Thinking out loud: comparing options, planning a trip or a launch, stress-testing an idea.
- Research: deep research mode reads many pages and returns a report you can then question.
- Files: summarizing a PDF, pulling figures from a spreadsheet, explaining a contract in plain words.
- Light coding: small scripts, formulas, explaining an error message. Heavier work goes to Codex, which is included with limits.

## Who it suits

**Free** is enough for people who use AI a few times a week for writing and quick questions.

**Go** suits students and casual users who keep hitting the free limits but don't need advanced reasoning. Accept the possibility of ads.

**Plus** is the plan for most working people: freelancers, marketers, small business owners, anyone who uses it daily. Projects, scheduled tasks and deeper research are where the value shows up.

**Pro** is for heavy users: researchers, developers and people who run long Codex or ChatGPT Work sessions all day.

**Business and Enterprise** are for companies. Business starts at two users.

## Pricing and plans (as of November 2026)

Prices below come from the [official pricing page](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/), checked on November 15, 2026. They are per user per month, before tax.

| Plan | Price | What you get, in short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | Unlimited text chats with GPT-6, limited uploads, images, voice, deep research and Codex |
| Go | Priced by country (see note) | More messages with tools, uploads, images and voice; longer memory; may include ads |
| Plus | $20/month | GPT-6.1 Sol and advanced reasoning, projects, scheduled tasks, custom GPTs, expanded deep research and Codex |
| Pro | From $100/month | Three usage tiers, GPT-6 Astra reasoning, Dot (OpenAI's always-on agent), maximum research and memory, up to $500 a month in API credits on the top tier |

A note on Go. OpenAI localizes the Go price in some markets, and the page shows a different amount depending on where you open it. When OpenAI [launched Go worldwide in January 2026](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-go/), it listed the US price at $8 a month. Check the pricing page from your own account before you subscribe.

Monthly API credits on Pro are tied to the tier: $100, $200 or $500. OpenAI says the credit benefit can change. Go, Plus and Pro are billed monthly by card. Business and Enterprise also have annual billing. OpenAI also offers ChatGPT for Teachers, free for verified US K–12 educators through June 2027, and up to 75% off Business or Enterprise for eligible nonprofits.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- The widest set of everyday features in one app
- A free plan that is useful on its own
- Plus covers most needs at a common price point for AI subscriptions
- Long context windows on paid plans

**Cons**

- Most limits are described in words, not numbers
- Go may show ads
- Personal-plan content is used for training unless you opt out
- Pro pricing has three tiers, which takes a minute to untangle

## The short verdict

Start on Free and use it for a real week of work. If you hit limits on uploads or research every day, Plus is the honest next step. Skip Go if ads would bother you, and skip Pro unless you already know you run long agent or coding sessions. Whatever plan you choose, open settings once and decide what you are comfortable sharing for training.
