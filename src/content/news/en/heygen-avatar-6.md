On Nov. 17, HeyGen released Avatar 6, its new talking-avatar model. The company says the model reads a script and performs it with gestures that match the line, rather than replaying the same motion loop, and that every frame is generated in native 2K. You can export in 1080p or 2K. HeyGen also lets you put two avatars in one frame and generate both sides of a conversation at once.

The number attached to the launch needs a closer look. HeyGen says that, side by side with real footage, Avatar 6 is "84.4% indistinguishable," up from 44.4% for Avatar V. That is not a simple poll in which 84.4% of viewers could not tell the videos apart. In HeyGen's own table, when people were shown a real recording and an Avatar 6 clip of the same sentence and audio, they picked the real one 57.8% of the time and Avatar 6 as real 42.2% of the time. The 84.4% figure is twice that 42.2% share. HeyGen's FAQ is explicit: 50% would mean raters cannot tell at all, "so Avatar 6 is 84.4% of the way there." When clips were shown alone, a real recording was judged real 78.9% of the time; Avatar 6 with the person's own voice was judged real 65.1%, and with a HeyGen voice, 56.5%. Those are HeyGen's figures, from tests it designed.

## What changed in the performance, and how you make one

HeyGen's pitch is that earlier avatars looked like you but recycled the same gestures. Avatar 6, it says, can point, count, and move in a direction that fits the line, and you can also prompt the emotion, expression, and gesture yourself. The company reports that it ranked first among nine commercial avatar products in a test judged on visual quality, identity, lip sync, motion, gesture-speech match, and artifacts, and that raters preferred Avatar 6 over Seedance 2.5 in 72.4% of side-by-side comparisons. Treat those as the company's research results.

To make a personal avatar, you upload a photo, verify that it is you, and record yourself talking. The how-to on the product page asks for at least 15 seconds; the FAQ says to record 30 seconds of video for an avatar of yourself. You then bring a script, upload audio, or start from a prompt, and generate the video. HeyGen says the first video is free, with no credit card, and that later use spends credits, with plans starting at $29 a month for 600 credits. The model is also available through HeyGen's API and MCP.

HeyGen's rules say only you, or someone you have clearly permitted, can make an avatar of you, and that making one without consent is not allowed. You can ask the company to remove your likeness.

## What this is useful for, and what it is not

If you already record explainers or product walkthroughs, Avatar 6 is a way to get a presenter on screen without a new shoot, with more control over gestures than the previous model. The "almost indistinguishable from a real person" headline is stronger than the study design supports, so it is worth watching the side-by-side tests yourself before you rely on that claim.

An avatar can walk through a product. It cannot fix a broken sign-in or a checkout that fails. If the site or app behind the video was built with Lovable, Bolt, Cursor, or v0, that last mile is still a production job.
