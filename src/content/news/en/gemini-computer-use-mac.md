On Nov. 19, Google added computer use to the Gemini app for Mac. You describe a job in plain language, and Gemini clicks, types, and works through menus and forms across your local files, desktop apps, and the web. It does that in an isolated window, so your main screen and cursor stay free. The feature is rolling out in English in the United States, for people 18 and older with a personal Google account on an AI Plus, Pro, or Ultra plan.

## How it works, and what you still approve

Google's pitch is that the repetitive work that normally takes dozens of clicks can be handed to Gemini while you keep typing, designing, or taking a call. In the blog post, the company says Gemini runs in a separate background window, with a live preview in the chat. You choose which apps it can access, watch every step, pause at any time, and approve key actions before they happen.

That last point is worth reading carefully. One paragraph in the announcement says you approve every action; another says you approve key actions. The safer reading is the second one: you stay in the loop for the important steps, but you should not assume that every click will stop and wait for you. Start with a narrow set of app permissions, and watch the preview on the first runs.

Google offers a few examples of what to try. Batch color-grade photos in a design app, or set up a complex 3D scene. Pull weights, dimensions, and addresses out of local PDFs to fill in a multi-box shipping quote. Take metrics and screenshots from live dashboards and drop them into a Google Slides deck. Analyze clothing photos in a local folder, research prices, and stage listings on a resale site. Those are Google's illustrations, not a test we ran.

## Who can use it today

The limits are specific. You need an Apple Silicon Mac running macOS Sequoia 15.0 or later. The rollout is in English in the U.S. It is available for personal Google accounts, for users 18 and older, on Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra. The announcement does not mention work or school accounts. To get the app, Google points to [gemini.google/mac](https://gemini.google/mac).

If you are outside the United States, on an Intel Mac, or on a regular free Gemini plan, this is not on your machine yet.

## What to try, and what not to hand over

If you already have a qualifying Mac and plan, the useful test is a chore you already know how to check: a folder of files to rename, a form you fill in every week, a deck that always starts from the same dashboard. Keep it off anything that can spend money, change a production system, or see a password manager. Give Gemini access only to the apps that task needs, and sit with the preview until you trust the pattern.

A helper that can click through your desktop is a different kind of risk than a chatbot. If it has permission to open a browser, it can reach the same logged-in sessions you can. That is the point of the feature, and also the reason to keep the first experiments small.

Computer use will not finish a product for you. If you are building a site or an app in Lovable, Bolt, Cursor, or v0, the last mile is still sign-in, payments, and a deploy that holds up, whether or not Gemini can click through a demo.
