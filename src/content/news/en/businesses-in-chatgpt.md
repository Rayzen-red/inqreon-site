On Nov. 10, OpenAI opened a beta called Businesses in ChatGPT. Companies can now claim their listing in ChatGPT, correct what it says about them, add product feeds with prices and stock, and see how often ChatGPT sends people their way. Eligible U.S. online stores and online service businesses can also publish a Business Agent, a chat assistant that answers shoppers' questions about their products and policies. If people already ask ChatGPT what to buy, this is the first official way to have a say in the answer.

## What Business Hub gives you

The control panel is called Business Hub, and it opens from the ChatGPT sidebar. You search for your business, pick the right listing, and prove you are connected to it. OpenAI's help center lists four ways: a code sent to the business phone, a code sent to a business email, a DNS TXT record on your website domain, or a connected Shopify store. After OpenAI approves the claim, the person managing the listing must also pass an identity check through Persona before publishing changes, viewing insights, or creating an agent.

Once verified, you can update key details and upload a product feed with pricing and availability. Shopify merchants can connect their store directly, and OpenAI notes that eligible Shopify products were already showing up in ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog.

The insights are deliberately coarse. VentureBeat reports that the dashboard covers the past 28 days: mentions, website clicks, and discovery topics, which are recurring themes in answers that cited the business. OpenAI says it does not share users' conversations or Business Agent chats, and that numbers appear only after activity passes certain thresholds. A blank metric does not mean zero activity, the help center adds.

One detail matters for anyone with a website. For an online business, a mention counts only when ChatGPT references the site. Naming the brand alone does not count.

## How a Business Agent works

When a business shows up in an answer, a user can open its ChatGPT profile and, if an agent is published, start a separate, labeled conversation with it. The business supplies the information and the rules, previews the agent with real customer questions, and publishes when the answers look right. OpenAI's example is a shopper comparing two products and asking which fits their needs.

The limits are clear in the help center. Today the agent cannot complete purchases, make bookings, or check an order. It explains products and policies and links to your website, where the customer finishes the job. Business Agents are available to eligible U.S. ecommerce and online service businesses with a website, within the industries allowed by OpenAI's Business Agents Policy. Local stores and restaurants can currently claim only an existing U.S. listing.

On cost, VentureBeat, citing Digiday, reports that claiming a profile and publishing an agent are free at launch, and that OpenAI's head of monetization left the door open to charging later. Businesses can also sponsor their agent as the destination for ChatGPT Ads, though VentureBeat notes that this ad format is in a limited test with selected advertisers. OpenAI says claiming a profile does not guarantee better placement, and ChatGPT keeps making its own recommendations.

## Why your website is still the main event

TechCrunch compared the launch to the business profiles Google has offered for years, and the comparison holds. A verified profile is a small step. The real work happens on the page the agent links to.

Look at what the beta rewards. Mentions for online businesses count only when ChatGPT cites your site. The agent sends buyers to your website to pay. Verification can run through a DNS record on your domain. All three assume a site that loads fast, has clear product and return pages, and a checkout that works on the first try.
