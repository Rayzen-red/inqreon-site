On Nov. 22, the Bengaluru company Sarvam released [Sarvam-105B-1711](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/sarvam-105b-1711), a new open-weight checkpoint of its 105-billion-parameter model. The company describes it as a Flash-Lite model for high-volume work such as customer support, voice agents, and agentic tasks. Compared with the February release, Sarvam says the new checkpoint follows instructions more reliably in long conversations, calls tools more accurately, and handles Indian languages far better. The weights are on Hugging Face under an Apache 2.0 license.

## What you can download, and what Sarvam claims

According to the [model card](https://huggingface.co/sarvamai/sarvam-105b-1711), Sarvam-105B-1711 uses the same base as Sarvam-105B: a mixture-of-experts model with 105 billion parameters and 10.3 billion active at a time. It was then post-trained for tool calling, instruction following, agentic tasks, and Indian languages. There are two public builds: a [BF16](https://huggingface.co/sarvamai/sarvam-105b-1711-bf16) set for further training, and an NVFP4 version of about 65 GB that, Sarvam writes, runs on a single workstation GPU. The company says the NVFP4 file is the same model it serves on its Token Factory API, and that support for serving it, including its DSpark speculative decoding, is already in vLLM.

The headline comparisons in the blog are Sarvam's own. Across eight conversational-agent benchmarks, it reports that the average rose from 42.6 in February to 73.9. Instruction following on IFBench, it says, went from 32.5 to 82.5; tool use in customer-service conversations on Tau2, from 46.2 to 85.5; and Indian-language performance on its own Indic suite, from 30.1 to 79.1. The new checkpoint, it adds, completed 69.5% of Tau2 tasks correctly in all four of four attempts, ahead of Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite at 63.9% and Gemma 4 31B at 62.1% in the same write-up.

Sarvam also places those scores next to price. It says it now offers the model at a blended $0.15 per million tokens, down from $0.44, and that this matches Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite's average of 74.0 at $0.85 and Gemma 4 31B's 73.0 at $0.54. Those figures, including the rival scores, come from Sarvam's own runs and its own price list. We have not repeated the tests.

The blog also describes a longer context. The company says it ran long-context training on 512 GPUs, processed 330 billion tokens in a little over seven days, and extended the model's context to 160,000 tokens. For anyone who wants to run it without the API, it points to a single RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU as a workable box for the NVFP4 build.

## What this changes if you actually ship something

An open checkpoint from a lab outside the usual US-China pair is useful on its face: you can try the API, or download the weights and run them yourself, under a permissive license. That still leaves the ordinary work. Sarvam is publishing its own averages and its own price comparison. If you care about a particular language, a particular tool, or a particular kind of customer-support conversation, those tables are a starting point, not a substitute for a test on your own tasks.

A model that calls tools more reliably still does not finish a site or an app. If you are assembling a product in Lovable, Bolt, Cursor, or v0 — or wiring a self-hosted model into one — the last mile is still review, sign-in, payments, and a deploy that holds up.
